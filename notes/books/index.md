# The Library — Book Catalog (Room 3)

This is the index of all known books found in Room 3 (The Library) of "Who Killed Uskar?". The library contains at least 26 catalogued books; additional volumes are present and will be documented as the game develops.

Books marked **[full text]** have a complete written text in this directory. All others are stubs — their titles, contents, and roles are defined, but no full text has been written yet.

---

## Philosophical Books

### On the Nature of Doors
**Description:** A treatise on the different types of doors, the constraints they place on souls passing through them, and the effects of time on their behavior.
**Relevance:** Provides foundational lore for door mechanics throughout the game — understanding which doors a soul can or cannot pass through is essential to navigation and puzzle-solving.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Subjectivity of Time
**Description:** An academic work on the discrete nature of time in this world, the structure of the time-tree, and how different beings perceive the passage of time differently.
**Relevance:** Core worldbuilding reference for the game's time mechanics; explains how time branching and time increments work from an in-world perspective.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Existence of Gods
**Description:** An authoritative account of the gods, the nature of possession, and the origin of magic as a power the Outer Gods placed into the world.
**Relevance:** Establishes the cosmological rules of the world — who the gods are, what they want, and why souls are subject to their influence. Relevant to understanding possession, sacrifice, and sin.
**Status:** Stub

---

### On Free Will
**Description:** A philosophical treatise examining whether creatures in this world possess genuine free will, or whether all action unfolds under divine control.
**Relevance:** Frames the central moral tension of the game — the question of guilt, responsibility, and agency. Directly relevant to investigating who killed Uskar and whether the killer had a choice.
**Status:** Stub

---

### On Murder as Cardinal Sin
**Description:** An examination of how the act of murder damages the soul of the killer, the spiritual weight it carries, and its implications in the divine order.
**Relevance:** Central to the mystery — the game's core crime is a murder. Understanding the spiritual mechanics of killing shapes how the player interprets clues and suspects.
**Status:** Stub

---

### On Deceit as Cardinal Sin
**Description:** A companion volume to the murder text, examining how lying, manipulation, and deception corrode the soul and invite divine judgment.
**Relevance:** Relevant to characters who may have deceived or withheld information. The Deceiver Daghal is the mythological archetype; this book provides the moral framework.
**Status:** Stub

---

### On the Sin of Splitting Time
**Description:** A condemnation of time-branching as a cardinal sin, detailing the metaphysical damage caused by deliberately fracturing the time-tree.
**Relevance:** Explains the stakes of the player's own time-manipulation actions. If the player splits time recklessly, this book hints at the consequences.
**Status:** Stub

---

### On the Sin of Morphing
**Description:** Distinguishes permanent morphing (a cardinal sin that distorts the soul beyond recovery) from temporary morphing, and explains why the former is condemned.
**Relevance:** Relevant to any character who has altered their form, and to understanding which transformations in the game carry moral weight.
**Status:** Stub

---

## Mechanics Books

### The Rites of Sacrifice
**Description:** A detailed account of the mechanics and rituals of willing soul sacrifice — the procedures, the conditions required, and the effects on the sacrificed soul.
**Relevance:** Directly teaches a core game mechanic. Players who read this book understand how voluntary sacrifice works and can recognize it when they encounter it.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Art of Magic Paintings
**Description:** An explanation of how magical paintings work, how they connect to the places they depict, and how altering the painting alters the place itself.
**Relevance:** Central mechanic reference for painting-based puzzles. Players need this knowledge to understand how modifying a painting changes the world.
**Status:** Stub

---

### Keys and Locks: The Architecture of Access
**Description:** A comprehensive guide to key types, lock types, spatial locks, and temporal locks — including how access can be constrained across time as well as space.
**Relevance:** Practical reference for lock-and-key puzzles throughout the game. Some locks operate temporally, and this book explains the rules.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Sword: Physical and Magical
**Description:** A guide to sword types, how swords scale with the soul of the wielder, and their use in magical as well as physical combat.
**Relevance:** Combat reference that also connects soul state to fighting ability — a player whose soul is damaged fights less effectively.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Dagger: Sinners' Tool
**Description:** A text on physical daggers, their association with sinful acts, and their history as the preferred weapon of those who operate outside divine law.
**Relevance:** Relevant to the murder investigation — the type of weapon used may carry moral or metaphysical significance. Potentially a clue.
**Status:** Stub

---

### On Dolls and Their Makers
**Description:** An account of demonic dolls, the sin associated with creating and using them, and their relationship to magic paintings as objects that bridge reality and representation.
**Relevance:** Connects two mechanics (dolls and paintings) and flags doll use as sinful — relevant to identifying suspects and understanding certain inventory items.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Power of Notes
**Description:** An explanation of how written notes function as world-altering documents in this world — how they are encoded, how they can be altered, and what effects changes to a note produce.
**Relevance:** Core mechanic reference. Notes are not passive objects; they can change the state of the world. Players who understand this can use notes as tools.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Inventory and Time
**Description:** A technical account of how the inventory behaves across time increments, including the rules that prevent artifact duplication when timelines branch or merge.
**Relevance:** Explains a potentially confusing mechanic — why certain items cannot be duplicated through time manipulation, and what happens to inventory when time splits.
**Status:** Stub

---

### Poisons and Their Purposes
**Description:** A survey of poison mechanics, the voluntary death cult that uses poison ritually, and the practical applications of poison in this world.
**Relevance:** Introduces poison as both a mechanic and a faction (the voluntary death cult). Relevant to cause-of-death investigation and to certain characters' motivations.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Shield: Block and Parry
**Description:** A practical guide to shield use in combat, distinguishing between blocking (absorbing force) and parrying (redirecting it), with notes on when each technique applies.
**Relevance:** Combat mechanic reference. Relevant to any combat encounter where the player must defend rather than attack.
**Status:** Stub

---

### On Monsters
**Description:** An account of what monsters are in this world, how they behave, and the spiritual significance of slaying them — specifically how killing a monster purifies the soul.
**Relevance:** Distinguishes morally between killing a monster and killing a person; important for understanding the soul-damage system and the divine purge.
**Status:** Stub

---

### Slaying Monsters: The Divine Purge
**Description:** A practical combat guide to fighting soulless creatures, with guidance on how the act of purging monsters contributes to the spiritual cleansing of the world.
**Relevance:** Companion volume to "On Monsters" — moves from philosophy to practice. Relevant to combat encounters and to understanding why certain enemies can be killed without sin.
**Status:** Stub

---

## Story and Mythological Books

### The Adventures of Gounargoun **[full text]**
**Description:** A mythological tale of a creature who fully surrendered to divine possession, abandoned all resistance to divine will, and ultimately achieved a state of nirvana.
**Relevance:** The positive archetype of divine surrender — contrasted with the horror of the demonic path (Zortaug). Thematically relevant to the game's central question of agency versus surrender.
**Status:** Full text available — [the_adventures_of_gounargoun.md](the_adventures_of_gounargoun.md)

---

### The Pilgrimage of Pyutkah **[full text]**
**Description:** The story of a seeker who attempted many paths toward divine connection and ultimately achieved it only through an act of genuine self-sacrifice.
**Relevance:** Explores self-sacrifice as the necessary price of transcendence; thematically relevant to the game's sacrifice mechanics and to understanding what certain characters may have been willing to do.
**Status:** Full text available — [the_pilgrimage_of_pyutkah.md](the_pilgrimage_of_pyutkah.md)

---

### The Horror of Zortaug
**Description:** A cautionary tale of a creature who rejected the divine path, chose demonic influence instead, and was gradually transformed into a monster beyond recognition or redemption.
**Relevance:** The negative archetype balancing Gounargoun's story. Establishes what happens when a soul chooses the wrong alignment — relevant to understanding monster origins and the soul-damage system.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Deceiver Daghal
**Description:** The story of a trickster who mastered the art of morphing and learned to exploit the willing sacrifices of others for personal gain. The outcome of the story is deliberately left unresolved.
**Relevance:** The archetype of deceit and manipulation. Relevant to "On Deceit as Cardinal Sin" and to any character in the game who has been using others. The unresolved ending is a narrative signal.
**Status:** Stub

---

### The Joy of Divine Possession
**Description:** A first-person account written by a being under divine possession, describing the experience as profound bliss and urging readers to surrender their will rather than resist.
**Relevance:** Propagandistic in tone — it presents divine possession as desirable, which frames the free will debate and raises questions about who benefits from souls accepting possession.
**Status:** Stub

---

### Sinali the Painter-Mage
**Description:** The story of a renowned painter who discovered the magical properties of her paintings, became a mage, and pioneered the art of using paintings to alter reality.
**Relevance:** Origin story for the magic painting mechanic. Understanding Sinali's history contextualizes how magic paintings came to exist in this world and who might know how to use them.
**Status:** Stub
