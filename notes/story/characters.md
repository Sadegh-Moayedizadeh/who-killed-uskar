# Characters — Who Killed Uskar?

This document is the canonical character bible for the game. All character names, spellings, and details here supersede earlier notes. The canonical spelling of the murdered individual is **USKAR** (not Uskerr — that was an early inconsistency in older notes).

---

## Uskar

The murdered individual. The game's central mystery and absent center.

### Identity and Role — the design truth **[CONFIRMED 2026-07-14; withheld in-game]**

Uskar is one of the base creature **Forms** — the Form of Inquiry, one of the fixed roster of first beings present in the First Room at T=0 (see [world/forms.md](../world/forms.md)). He held the largest soul the world has ever contained, committed the world's first sin (the frontier-minting morph that created the second room), wrote the first notes, became the first mage by purifying himself back to maximum soundness — and is the only being ever to have **exited time**.

Uskar was not murdered. His decision tree — the only tree in the world with a terminal node — halted. Before exiting, he staged his "death" at T_d, Chronos-locked his testament to that moment, stamped the First Room with the question "Who killed Uskar?" as a lure for gods, and left. The murder the game's title asks about never happened; the question is the summoning mechanism for the Outer God the player plays. His full decision tree is specified in [world/forms.md](../world/forms.md).

**In-game, all of this is deliberately unknown at the start.** To the player and the world's creatures, Uskar is a murdered being of extraordinary significance whose death set the game's events in motion. The ambiguity — god, legendary mage, creature of rare purity — should be preserved until the game earns the revelation, and each partial truth discovered in Act 4 should remain compatible with all three candidate answers until Act 5.

### Thematic Significance

Uskar is the absent center. Every character, room, clue, and motivation in the game orbits them. They never appear in person — they are gone when the game begins — yet their presence is felt in every discovery the player makes. Uskar functions as the hole in the world that the story keeps falling into. That the hole is an exit, not a wound, is the game's final revelation.

## The Original Vessel

The player's first controlled character. Their name and origin are deliberately withheld at the start.

### Identity and Background

A creature of ambiguous background found in Room 1 with no memory of how they arrived or why they are there. **[CONFIRMED 2026-07-14]** The vessel is a far-generation duplicate of **Uskar's own line** — it carries Uskar's Form. Silarmousch selected and placed it in Room 1 deliberately: bait of Uskar's Form, to fit Uskar's hook. The god summoned by Uskar's question would investigate Uskar's death while wearing a body of Uskar's lineage — which also means any witness who later describes "a figure with Uskar's face" may be describing Uskar, Silarmousch, or the player's vessel. The ambiguity is structural and intentional (see the witness mechanics in [narrative.md](narrative.md)).

In-game, the vessel's identity is withheld at the start and resolved during Act 4.

### Fate

They die in Room 4 by drinking Bol's poison nectar. Their death is the transition point at which the Outer God (the player) passes into Bol as the new vessel.

### Gameplay Role

The player's first vessel. Controlled through Rooms 1 to 4. Their death ends the first phase of the game.

### Thematic Significance

The original vessel represents the disposable instrument — a creature used, moved, sinned through, and discarded. Their unresolved identity invites the question of whether any vessel is truly known or simply a vehicle for forces larger than themselves.

## Lisspeg

A simple, earnest creature who waited faithfully in Room 1. Of **Gounargoun's lineage** — the Form of Surrender; his uncomplicated faith is the ancestral tree expressing itself purely.

### Personality

Devout, naive, genuinely good-hearted. Lisspeg believes deeply in prophecy and divine purpose. He is not sophisticated. He is not strategic. He is simply faithful and full of love for whatever higher purpose he has been entrusted with.

### Role

Silarmousch gave Lisspeg a key and told him that a "promised prophet" would come. Lisspeg waited. When the player (original vessel) arrives, Lisspeg is overcome with joy. He calls them the prophet. He hands over the key willingly, without hesitation or suspicion.

### Fate

Killed by the player (original vessel) in Room 1 with the Sinner's Dagger. This is the game's first cardinal sin. Lisspeg does not resist. He does not fight back. He dies with his trust intact and his faith unbroken.

### Future Possibility

Later in the game, the player — now as an Outer God — may return through time, possess Lisspeg's soul, and play as him. This would reveal Lisspeg's unique perspective on events the player already witnessed, and open paths that were closed to the original vessel.

### Thematic Significance

Lisspeg represents innocence instrumentalized by manipulative forces. He is good, and his goodness is the very thing that makes him useful as a tool. His death is the game's moral inciting incident — the moment the player is made complicit in something they cannot undo.

## Silarmousch

A powerful mage. The architect of the demonic trap in Rooms 1 through 3.

### Origin **[CONFIRMED 2026-07-14]**

Silarmousch is a **direct duplicate of Uskar** — minted when Uskar split time to witness the first possession (Gounargoun's) and arrived displaced from his past self (see the creation history in [world/forms.md](../world/forms.md)). He carries Uskar's Form, Uskar's face, and a variant of Uskar's decision tree — with a smaller soul.

That smaller soul is his tragedy and his motive. Uskar's tree is the only tree with a terminal node: the exit from time, reachable only at maximum soundness. Silarmousch's duplication cost him capacity — his REPAIR rule can never reach the maximum, so the halt node is *permanently unreachable for him*, yet his inherited model contains the proof that it exists. He is a seeker who cannot halt: a mind holding the proof of its own imprisonment. His founding memory, inherited from the moment of his minting, is watching a god take a creature.

### Personality

Cold, calculating, masterful. Silarmousch does not act directly when indirect manipulation will suffice. He designs systems — social, metaphysical, spatial — and lets others walk into them. He is not impulsive. Every piece he places has a purpose.

### Actions

- Placed Lisspeg in Room 1, gave him a key, and told him a prophet would come.
- Planted the Sinner's Dagger in Room 2.
- Designed Room 3's demonic path.

### Motives **[CONFIRMED 2026-07-14 — design truth; ambiguous in-game through Act 4]**

Silarmousch cannot exit time; the next-best unreachability is **not existing on the branch the perceiving god occupies**. His plan, which falls out of running his own clamped decision tree: steer the summoned Outer God into travelling back to the primordial past and killing Uskar *before the time-split that minted Silarmousch*. On the resulting branch, the duplication never happens and Silarmousch never exists — and a creature that does not exist on the god's branch cannot be located, perceived, or possessed by that god. The god's own act of murder splits time (a cardinal sin, corrupting the god's soul), and a Chronos-locked anchor planted in the god's inventory strands it on the new branch. Corruption is the means; self-erasure into an unreachable branch is the end.

He cannot do the deed himself: if Silarmousch kills Uskar, the god remains on the original branch beside him and nothing is gained. Only the god's own act strands the god. He must manipulate, never act — which is his entire character. See [narrative.md](narrative.md) ("Silarmousch's Escape Plan") for the full mechanism, including the mercy pitch.

In-game, his motive should read as ambiguous through Act 4 — corruption-for-its-own-sake, liberation-of-creatures, and private-agenda readings should all stay credible until the truth is earned.

### Current Status

Unknown. Likely still active in the world, watching from a distance.

### Thematic Significance

Silarmousch represents the misuse of knowledge — the cold application of intelligence toward opaque ends. He understands systems deeply enough to corrupt them without appearing to touch them. He is the manipulator who is never in the room when the harm happens.

## Bournache

A divine mage. The counter-force to Silarmousch's trap. Of **Pyutkah's lineage** — the Form of Devotion, whose primordial deed was the first willing sacrifice. Bournache's death is his Form's signature act expressing itself again.

### Personality

Wise, selfless, principled. Bournache does not value his own life above what he believes is right. He is not reckless — his sacrifice is deliberate and considered. He acts out of conviction, not impulse.

### Actions

- Altered Room 3 via a magical painting, redirecting its nature.
- Left an unmissable note to warn the player.
- Appears in Room 3 to offer his sacrifice in person.
- Prior to his own death, arranged for Bol to be positioned in Room 4 with the poison nectar.

### Sacrifice

Bournache gives his entire soul to the player's vessel, purifying it — and dies. He chose this.

### Status

Dead by the time the player reaches Room 4.

### Thematic Significance

Bournache represents divine grace and willing sacrifice. His death mirrors the game's broader theme of purification through loss. Where Silarmousch builds traps, Bournache builds exits. His sacrifice is the answer to the question Silarmousch's trap asks.

## Bol

A young apprentice mage. The player's second vessel, from Room 4 onward. Of **Sinali's lineage** — the Form of Making; his earnest craft and his affinity for the mage's arts are the ancestral tree showing through.

### Personality

Earnest, curious, slightly anxious. Bol is not naive — he has a mage's education and genuine talent — but he is young enough that the weight of extraordinary events still shows on him. He is genuinely fond of his teachers.

### Background

Trained first by Bournache; now mentored by Sekmidorgue. He is serious about his craft and his relationships. He did not choose to be part of any of this.

### Role

Placed in Room 4 by Bournache's prior arrangement, carrying the poison nectar. He does not know the nectar will kill the original vessel — or he understands that it will cause them to pass beyond, which he interprets as "making the door visible" in a spiritual sense. Either reading holds. He gives the nectar without deception as he understands it.

### Effect of Possession

When the Outer God takes Bol, the possession is total: Bol loses all will and consciousness for as long as he is held, and he is not released until the vessel dies. The strength and purity of his soul make him an excellent vessel — a clean channel with full access to magic.

> **Resolved (2026-07-14).** Under total possession, Bol's arc lands on those who *witness* him, and on Bol himself only at the moment of release. During Acts 3–4, the marks of carrying a god are observable by others, not by Bol: Sekmidorgue and the Act 4 mages react to what Bol has visibly become — clearer, more powerful, slightly wrong. Bol experiences none of it. His arc completes in the true ending: the only way a possession has ever ended without the vessel dying is the god *leaving the world entirely*, and if the player takes the exit in Act 5, **Bol wakes — the only vessel in history released alive**. He comes back to himself in Room 1, at the beginning, holding a signed note that answers a question he never heard asked. See [narrative.md](narrative.md), Act 5.

### Thematic Significance

Bol represents the vessel as gift and casualty at once. He did not ask to carry a god, and he is absent for every moment of the carrying; his story is told in the reactions of the people who knew him. His reward — in the true ending — is being given back to himself.

## Sekmidorgue

A high mage at the Altar of Mages. Of **Gounargoun's lineage** — the Form of Surrender; his reverence for divine possession as a sacred circumstance is his ancestral tree speaking.

### Personality

Stern, formal, but genuinely invested in Bol's development. Sekmidorgue's discipline is not coldness — it is the form his care takes. He has standards because he respects his students enough to hold them to something.

### Role

Primary teacher and guide during the tutorial phase of Rooms 5 through 10. He knows that Bol is now carrying an Outer God. He treats this as extraordinary and sacred — not alarming, not threatening, but requiring careful navigation.

### Status

Alive and present as of Room 5.

## The Forms (Historical Figures)

**[CONFIRMED 2026-07-14]** These figures are referenced in myth and NPC dialogue as legends — and the legends are accurate; they are just old. These are the base creature **Forms**: the first beings, one of each, present in the First Room at T=0. Every creature in the game descends from one of them (see [world/forms.md](../world/forms.md) for the full cosmology and each Form's primordial deed). Uskar is the sixth Form; his entry is at the top of this file. In-world, NPCs believe these are mythological figures — the truth of their nature is late-game material.

### Gounargoun

The Form of Surrender. The first creature ever possessed by an Outer God — he surrendered utterly, performed wonders, and his story ends in nirvana: complete dissolution into the divine. Invoked in myth as the ideal of what total surrender to possession can become. His possession is also the event Uskar split time to witness — the split that minted Silarmousch.

### Pyutkah

The Form of Devotion. She sought to meet a god through pilgrimages across her entire life and never managed it through effort or intention. Finally, through the first willing sacrifice in history, the connection was achieved — revealing purification physics to the world and to the watching gods. Her story is a parable about the limits of seeking and the necessity of release. The cult of mass suicide is a corrupted branch of her lineage.

### Zortaug

The Form of Refusal. He refused ever to be taken by a god and corroded his own soul deliberately, sin by sin, until the link severed — the first monster, and the archetype every future monster repeats. The counterpoint to Gounargoun: the same crossroads, the opposite choice, the opposite end. Canonically, Uskar himself later slew Zortaug — the first seeker giving the first refuser the only mercy left.

### Daghal

The Form of Exploitation. A trickster who extracted the general rule of the young world — passage makes places, returning makes copies — and invented duplication-farming, filling the world with objects and shredding his own soul without caring. He duplicated himself, and the first lie in history was Daghal deceiving his own copy. Current status unknown. The unresolved ending is deliberate: some cautionary figures don't have clean endings.

### Sinali

The Form of Making. The first painter-mage: she bound an image to a place and discovered the Painting Form's kinship with the Room Form — that a painting is not representation but presence. Her earliest canvases are bound to the First Room at the dawn values of time and hang, ages later, on the walls of Room 1. The meadow image — the game's recurring glimpse of outside-time — is hers. Bournache's alteration of Room 3 draws on her tradition; Bol is of her lineage.
