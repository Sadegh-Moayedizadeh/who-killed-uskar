# Room 7

## Label

Can the mind touch the distant?

## Description

An ornate circular art gallery, wide and high-ceilinged, with polished stone floors that catch the light from sconces mounted between each painting frame. The walls curve inward at the top, giving the room the feeling of an eye looking upward. Every surface that is not a doorway is devoted to display: dozens of framed works hang at even intervals, each one a careful, detailed scene rendered in oils that seem slightly too vivid, slightly too present, to be purely decorative.

Most of the paintings here are inert — beautiful but passive. The player will likely not be able to tell, at first, which ones are magical and which are not. That ambiguity is part of the room's design. Sekmidorgue sends the player here to learn the difference, and more specifically to learn what can be done once the difference is understood.

At the center of the room sits a physical bronze chest. It does not look like part of the gallery's collection. It sits on the floor with the matter-of-fact solidity of a thing that has always been there, sealed by a heavy iron lock. There is no key anywhere in the room. Any attempt to force the chest — through strength, leverage, or whatever combat tools the player may be carrying — will fail completely. The chest cannot be broken or removed. It simply exists, waiting.

On the far wall, directly opposite the entrance, hangs a painting that stands apart from the others. Its subject is immediately recognizable: a small bronze chest resting in a sunlit meadow, sealed by a heavy iron lock that is identical in every detail to the one on the chest in the center of the room. The resemblance is not coincidental. The resemblance is the point.

## Contents

### Artifacts

**Painting "The Locked Chest"**

A magical painting hanging on the far wall of the gallery. It depicts a small bronze chest sitting in a sunlit meadow, its lid held shut by a heavy iron lock. The scene is rendered in precise, unambiguous detail — the meadow is open and still, the light is clear, and the chest and its lock are the undisputed subject of the composition. Nothing else in the image demands attention.

The painting is magically bound to the physical chest at the center of the room. This binding is not visible, not labeled, and not announced. The player must piece it together from observation: the chest in the painting and the chest in the room are the same object. Whatever is done to the depiction propagates to the reality.

**Physical Bronze Chest**

A real bronze chest sitting on the floor at the center of the room. Sealed by an iron lock that matches the one in the painting in every detail. The chest is indestructible and cannot be opened by any physical means — no key, no crowbar, no weapon, no force. It will not yield to anything applied directly to it. The only way to open it is through the painting.

Inside the chest is the Scroll of Insight.

**Brush of Alteration**

A slender wooden brush with fine dark bristles. It does not look exceptional, and in any other hands it would not be — but for a creature with a sufficiently pure soul, it is a tool of direct magical intervention. A soul that is pure enough can wield the Brush to alter the contents of a magical painting: adding elements, removing them, or changing what is depicted. The alteration is not cosmetic. Whatever the Brush changes in a magical painting, the corresponding reality changes with it.

Sekmidorgue provides the Brush of Alteration to the player before they enter Room 7. Without it, the puzzle cannot be solved. With it, the puzzle resolves in a single act, provided the player understands what to do.

**Scroll of Insight**

Contained inside the physical bronze chest. A scroll that, upon reading, grants the reader a deep and intuitive understanding of remote manipulation — the principle by which an action taken at one location produces an effect at another. The Scroll does not merely describe this principle in abstract terms; reading it produces a felt comprehension, a structural change in how the reader perceives the relationship between depiction and reality, cause and effect, the near and the distant.

Reading the Scroll also further purifies the reader's soul. It is both a reward for solving the puzzle and a preparation for what follows.

### Doors

**Door 10 — to Room 5**

Two-way passage between Room 7 and Room 5 (the Altar of Mages). No time change on passage in either direction. This is the only door in the room.

## Puzzle

The chest at the center of the room cannot be opened by any direct means. There is no key, and the chest itself is indestructible. The player must find a way to open the lock without touching it.

The solution is the Brush of Alteration applied to the Painting "The Locked Chest."

The player uses the Brush to erase or open the iron lock as it appears in the painting — removing it from the depicted scene, or painting it in an open position. Because the painting is magically bound to the physical chest, this alteration propagates immediately: the real lock clicks open in the center of the room. The chest can now be lifted and the Scroll of Insight retrieved from inside.

The puzzle teaches the foundational principle of magical paintings: the depiction and the reality are not separate things. The painting does not represent the chest — it is bound to the chest. Changing one changes the other. This is the mechanic the room exists to demonstrate, and it is stated in no uncertain terms by what happens the moment the Brush touches the canvas and the lock across the room falls open.

## Entry Flows

### Regular Entry

The player enters Room 7 from Room 5 via Door 10, carrying the Brush of Alteration provided by Sekmidorgue. The gallery surrounds them: curved walls hung with paintings, most of them ordinary, one of them not.

The bronze chest in the center of the room is visible immediately. Any attempt to open it by physical means will fail. The chest offers no purchase — it cannot be forced, broken, or moved. There is no key to find. The room does not explain this; it simply presents the fact of the sealed chest and leaves the player to look elsewhere.

Examining the paintings on the walls, the player will eventually reach the far wall and find "The Locked Chest." The resemblance to the physical chest is exact and unmistakable: same proportions, same bronze surface, same heavy iron lock. A player carrying the Brush of Alteration, having been told by Sekmidorgue that the Brush can alter magical paintings, has everything needed to form the hypothesis: alter the painting, change the chest.

Applying the Brush of Alteration to the iron lock in the painting — erasing it, opening it, removing it from the scene — causes the physical lock in the center of the room to click open. The chest is now accessible. The player opens it and retrieves the Scroll of Insight.

Reading the Scroll grants deep understanding of remote manipulation and further purifies Bol's soul. With both effects absorbed, the player returns to Room 5 via Door 10 to report to Sekmidorgue.

The label resolves on the way out. The mind touched the distant. It did so through the painting, through the Brush, through the link between depiction and reality that this room was built to teach. The answer to the question is not a statement of principle — it is the open chest sitting in the middle of the floor.
