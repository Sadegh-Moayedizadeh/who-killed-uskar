# Room 9

## Label

Does faith hold weight?

## Description

A grand armory carved into the outer wall of the Altar of Mages — long, vaulted, and lined floor to ceiling with weapons displayed on iron brackets. The room is lit entirely by the weapons themselves. Every blade, every haft, every pommel gives off a faint luminescence — some cold white, some a deep amber, some an uneasy blue-green — and the combined effect is a light that is steady but sourceless, casting no shadows in any direction. The air carries a low hum that the player will feel in their chest before they hear it.

The weapons lining the walls are magical. Not enchanted in the decorative sense — not sharper for their markings or heavier for their age — but magical in the precise and inconvenient sense: they respond to the soul of whoever holds them. A weapon in these racks is not a fixed instrument. It is a relationship. What it becomes in the hand depends entirely on what the hand brings to it.

Most players, arriving in this room for the first time, will not yet understand what that means in practical terms. The room is designed to make it understood in practical terms.

At the far end of the armory stands the Armored Husk — a suit of plate armor, standing upright, animated by the residual magic that saturates every surface in this room. It does not move until approached. It does not breathe, does not speak, does not give any indication of intent. It simply occupies space at the end of the room the way a locked door does: as an obstacle whose terms have not yet been established.

The armory was not built as a trial in the punishing sense. Sekmidorgue designed it to demonstrate a principle so central to the mechanics of this world that it needed to be demonstrated rather than described. The room's label is a question. By the time the player leaves, the answer will be a physical fact they observed with their own hands.

## Contents

### Creatures

**Armored Husk**

An animated suit of thick plate armor, held together and given motion by the residual magical energy that has accumulated in the armory over many years of housing magical weapons. There is no creature inside the armor. The suit itself is the creature — its joints move, its gauntlets grip, its helm turns to track movement — but it has no body, no organs, no will in any meaningful sense. It is a concentration of ambient magic that has taken on the shape of a soldier.

The Husk is immune to physical damage. Striking it with an ordinary weapon — a training sword, a cudgel, a fist — produces no effect. The blow lands, the metal rings, and nothing happens. It is also immune to demonic artifacts: objects powered by or aligned with corrupted or sinful energy pass through its resistance entirely. The Husk's composition is purely magical, and corrupted magic interacts with it in the same way a shadow interacts with a wall.

Only a magical weapon wielded by a sufficiently pure soul can damage it. This is not a conditional rule — it is a description of what the Husk is. The Husk is, in essence, a lock. The Blade of the Zealous in the hands of a purified soul is the key.

In combat, the Husk moves deliberately and telegraphs its attacks. It is not designed to punish. It is designed to survive long enough for the player to discover and exhaust every ineffective option before finding the one that works, so that when the Blade of the Zealous first connects and the armor shudders and the light in the room shifts — that moment is earned by the failure that preceded it.

### Artifacts

**Blade of the Zealous**

A sword displayed alone on a stand near the armory's entrance, slightly apart from the ranked weapons on the walls. To look at it, the player will notice nothing extraordinary. The blade is plain, the crossguard unadorned, the grip wrapped in something dark and worn. It does not glow more brightly than the weapons around it.

Picking it up is where the distinction becomes material.

In the hands of a creature whose soul is compromised by sin, the Blade of the Zealous is a poor weapon: heavier than it should be for its size, the edge dull, the balance wrong. It swings with effort and connects without authority. A sinner carrying this sword is carrying dead weight that happens to be blade-shaped.

In the hands of a creature whose soul is pure, the transformation is immediate and complete. The sword becomes weightless. The edge becomes sharp beyond any physical sharpening — not because the blade changes shape, but because the soul holding it changes what the blade can do. The sword is not performing at a higher level. It is performing at the level it was made for, which was only ever accessible to a soul clean enough to reach it.

The player, arriving in Room 9 after slaying the Chained Monsters in Room 6 and reading the Scroll of Insight in Room 7, has been purifying Bol's soul across those encounters. Bol is not perfectly pure — the game does not demand perfection. But the soul is sufficiently clean that the Blade of the Zealous responds. It comes to life in Bol's hand. The player will feel this before they understand it.

### Doors

**Door to Room 5**

Two-way passage between Room 9 and Room 5 (the Altar of Mages). No time change on passage in either direction. This is the only door in the room.

## Entry Flows

### Regular Entry

The player enters Room 9 from Room 5, likely having completed the lessons in Rooms 6, 7, and 8, and carrying the Training Blade from Room 6. The armory opens up around them — the weapon-light, the hum, the rows of glowing blades on either side.

The Armored Husk is visible at the far end of the room. The player approaches and engages with the Training Blade. The blade strikes the armor and produces nothing. No chip, no dent, no reaction from the Husk. The player hits it again. Still nothing. The Husk, for its part, begins to respond — it moves, it swings, it exerts pressure — but it does not fall.

The player surveys the room. The Blade of the Zealous is near the entrance, on its stand. It is picked up. In Bol's hand, because Bol's soul has been purified by the work of the preceding rooms, the blade is light and sharp and immediately different from the Training Blade in a way that is hard to articulate but easy to feel.

The player returns to the Husk and strikes it. The armor shudders. The luminescent light in the room responds — a brief intensification, a flicker. The Husk sustains damage. A few more strikes and it shatters: the plates come apart, the animating magic disperses, and what was standing at the end of the armory is now scattered across the floor.

The player returns to Room 5 via the door at the armory's entrance.

### Design Note

The label asks a question that the rest of the game has been circling since Room 6 without yet stating directly: *Does faith hold weight?*

The answer the room gives is not philosophical. It is mechanical. The Blade of the Zealous is heavier in a sinner's hand — literally, physically heavier. The Armored Husk cannot be damaged by corrupted tools — not as a rule imposed from outside, but as a consequence of what both the Husk and the corruption fundamentally are. A creature's inner state does not merely affect how they interact with the world. It determines what the world will allow them to do.

This is the game's central metaphysical principle made explicit in mechanical terms. Everything in Rooms 6 through 8 asked the player to trust that soul purity mattered, that the incremental work of killing the Chained Monsters and reading the Scroll of Insight was building toward something. Room 9 is where that trust is cashed out. The sword that was heavy becomes weightless. The creature that was invulnerable falls. Faith — or whatever name is given to the state of a soul that has done the work of becoming clean — does not merely have weight. It is the only thing in this room with any weight at all.
