# Room 8

## Label

What hides in plain sight?

## Description

A quiet, dusty scriptorium annexed off the Altar of Mages — a room that looks, at first glance, like it has nothing to teach. The shelves are deep and crowded with ledgers. The air is still. A single lantern burns in the corner, casting a warm and even light across the room that makes everything feel legible, open, known.

It is not.

Sheets of parchment drift slowly through the air without falling, loosed from their bindings by decades of accumulated magical residue. Some carry visible text. Some appear blank. Some carry text that, to the unprepared eye, appears to be nothing more than random marks — decorative flourish, old water damage, the scrawl of a mage whose pen had wandered. The room's lesson begins with this: not every surface that appears blank is empty, and not every surface that appears legible is telling the truth.

The scriptorium was used by mages who understood that certain truths were too dangerous or too sacred to leave fully exposed. They developed systems of encoding — not ciphers in the mechanical sense, but soul-keyed obscurations. A note could be written in a way that rendered its contents unreadable to any reader whose soul was not aligned with the intent of the writer. The information was present. It was simply hidden from those not prepared to receive it.

The lantern in the corner has burned here for a very long time. This, too, is part of the room's design, though that will not become clear until the player has read what the room has left for them.

## Contents

### Artifacts

**Encoded Note — "The Unseen Path"**

A single sheet of parchment, settled on the central writing desk as though placed deliberately — which it was. Left behind by a past mage who understood that not all information should be immediately accessible. To the naked eye, the note is illegible: its surface is covered in characters that shift and blur as the eye tries to settle on them, resolving into nothing consistent. It cannot be read without assistance. Attempting to do so produces only the vague impression of meaning — close enough to feel significant, too unstable to be acted on.

The note is the room's primary object. Everything else exists in relation to it.

**Cipher Lens**

A magical magnifying glass, old and carefully maintained, left in the scriptorium by Sekmidorgue for this lesson. When held and turned toward an encoded note, it translates the obscured text — but not unconditionally. The Cipher Lens is soul-keyed: it reads the soul of the creature holding it and reveals only what that soul is pure enough to receive. A compromised soul will receive a partial translation, or none at all. A pure soul receives the full text.

At this stage in the tutorial, the player's soul is intact. The Cipher Lens held over the Encoded Note resolves the shifting characters into legible words.

The decrypted note reads: *The lantern in the corner burns bright, blinding the way.*

This is not metaphor. It is a literal description of a literal condition in this room.

**Alteration Quill**

A magical quill, also provided by Sekmidorgue. It looks unremarkable — worn, slightly heavy, the feather a deep grey-black. Its function is precise and significant: the Alteration Quill allows the holder to rewrite specific keywords in a decrypted note, and in doing so, alter the local reality the note describes.

The alteration is not unlimited. The Quill acts on what is written, not on the writer's intentions in isolation — the text must exist and be decrypted before it can be changed. The change must be plausible within the note's internal logic. And the magic operates locally: a note altered in this room affects conditions in this room. The implications for what might be done with notes encountered elsewhere in the game are left unstated, but they are present.

### Doors

**Door 11 — to Room 5**

Two-way passage between Room 8 and Room 5 (the Altar of Mages). No time change on passage in either direction. This is the only door in the room.

## Puzzle

The puzzle is contained entirely within the relationship between the three artifacts and the physical environment of the room.

1. The player picks up the Cipher Lens and holds it over the Encoded Note. Because the player's soul is pure, the Lens translates the full text. The note reads: *The lantern in the corner burns bright, blinding the way.*

2. The player observes the lantern in the corner. It is burning. The room is evenly lit. There is nothing on the far wall that appears significant — no marking, no door, no sigil.

3. The player picks up the Alteration Quill. The decrypted note is now editable. The player changes the word "bright" to "dark."

4. The physical lantern in the corner extinguishes. The room falls into near-darkness.

5. On the far wall — invisible while the lantern burned, its glow drowned out by the surrounding light — a sigil pulses into visibility. It was there the entire time. The lantern's light was not illuminating the room; it was concealing this.

6. The glowing sigil marks the completion of the room's lesson. The player returns to Room 5 via Door 11.

The puzzle has no trick components and no wrong paths. The room is designed so that each artifact is found in the order it needs to be used, and each step produces an unambiguous observable result. The point is not difficulty — it is demonstration.

## Entry Flows

### Regular Entry

The player enters Room 8 from Room 5 via Door 11, having been assigned the scriptorium lesson by Sekmidorgue and given no starting artifact for this room — the room's tools are already inside it.

The encoded note is immediately visible on the writing desk. Attempting to read it without the Cipher Lens produces nothing useful — the text shifts and refuses to settle.

The player finds the Cipher Lens and holds it over the note. The text resolves. The note describes the lantern in terms that suggest it is an obstacle rather than an aid. The player finds the Alteration Quill, edits the decrypted text — "bright" becomes "dark" — and the lantern goes out.

The sigil on the far wall emerges from the darkness, glowing steadily. The room's question — *What hides in plain sight?* — is answered by the act of answering it: the sigil was there from the moment the player entered. The lantern's brightness was the concealment.

The player returns to Room 5 via Door 11.

### Design Note

The room establishes two principles that carry forward through the rest of the game.

The first is that truth is not always inaccessible — it may simply require the right instrument and a soul prepared to receive it. The Cipher Lens does not create information; it reveals what was already written. The soul-purity requirement is not an arbitrary gate. It reflects the mage's intent: some truths should not reach everyone.

The second is that notes, once decrypted, can be altered — and altered notes change the world they describe. The Alteration Quill works here, in this room, on this note, producing a local and specific effect. But the mechanic is not limited to Room 8. The player has now learned that written descriptions of reality are not fixed, and that changing a word in a note is not an act of editing but an act of intervention. What this means for notes encountered in other rooms, in other contexts, under other conditions — that is left for the player to discover.
