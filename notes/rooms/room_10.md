# Room 10

## Label

Can a moment be caged?

## Description

A crystalline vault, unlike any other space in the Altar of Mages. The walls are not stone but pure translucent mineral, grown rather than cut, and behind them something moves — not light exactly, but the impression of light, a shimmering presence in the material itself that pulses with slow, rhythmic irregularity. The air carries a faint charge. Every breath feels slightly thicker than it should, as if the atmosphere here is denser with time than elsewhere, as if more of it is present than usual.

This is the final tutorial room. The others taught the player how to fight, how to read, how to look, how to arm themselves. This one teaches the hardest concept of all: that time is not merely a condition the player moves through but a dimension that can be manipulated, locked, and exploited the way space can. The crystalline structure of the room is not decorative. It reflects and amplifies temporal energy, making the mechanics here visible in ways they would not otherwise be. The shimmer in the walls is time — or more precisely, it is the visible signature of temporal binding, the residue left by magic that has pinned objects to specific integer moments.

At the center of the room sits a stone pedestal. On the pedestal rests the Purified Chalice, enclosed in a Chronos Lock. Invisible defensive magic surrounds the pedestal, keyed to the presence of the Chalice and calibrated to respond the moment any hand reaches for it. The trap does not announce itself. It will not become apparent until the player tries to take the Chalice and discovers, from the consequences, that something is protecting it.

The Chronos Lock is the room's central object. It is what everything here is designed to explain. It binds its contents not to a location but to a moment — a specific integer value of time. An object locked by the Chronos Lock is only real, only accessible, only present, at the time value it was locked into. At any other moment, it does not exist. It cannot be touched, triggered, or targeted, because from any other temporal vantage point there is nothing there to touch.

The room's question is answered by the room itself. The Purified Chalice is caged in a moment. That is the lesson.

## Contents

### Artifacts

**Chronos Lock**

A specialized lock, ancient and precisely made, unlike any conventional locking mechanism. Its function is not spatial but temporal: when closed around an object, it binds that object to the exact integer time value the lock occupies at the moment of closure. From that point forward, the object exists — physically, magically, perceptibly — only at that time value. At any other integer moment, the locked object is absent. It cannot be seen, touched, retrieved, or acted upon. The defensive magic protecting the Chalice is similarly bound to the Chalice's presence; when the Chalice does not exist, the trap has nothing to protect and lies dormant.

The Chronos Lock on the pedestal was closed at time T. The Purified Chalice inside it exists at T and only at T.

**Temporal Key**

The only implement capable of opening a Chronos Lock. It does not function as an ordinary key functions — it does not simply turn in a mechanism. It is specifically constructed to operate on temporal bindings, and it will open a Chronos Lock only when the holder is present at the same time value the lock was closed. The Temporal Key is present in the room at time T+1, placed there by design as part of the lesson's architecture. The player must locate it and understand when it can be used.

**The Purified Chalice**

A sacred artifact. Sekmidorgue requires its return as proof that the lesson has been understood and completed — not merely that the player has retrieved it, but that the retrieval was accomplished correctly, which is to say without triggering the trap. A Chalice claimed through brute force or by circumventing rather than understanding the temporal lock would not satisfy the requirement. The method of acquisition is the demonstration.

At time T, the Chalice rests on the pedestal, enclosed in the Chronos Lock, surrounded by dormant defensive magic that will activate the instant the Chalice is touched. At time T+1, the Chalice does not exist. The pedestal is empty. The trap is dormant not by being disarmed but by having nothing to guard.

### Doors

**Door 12 — Time Loop Door (to center of Room 10)**

One-way. Does not lead out of the room. Passing through Door 12 returns the player to the center of Room 10, but increments time by exactly +1 unit. It is the primary mechanism for shifting temporal state within this room. Repeated passage through Door 12 would continue incrementing time; the puzzle requires only a single use.

**Door 13 — to Room 5**

Two-way passage between Room 10 and Room 5 (the Altar of Mages). No time change on passage in either direction. This is the room's only exit to the broader tutorial hub.

## Puzzle

The player enters Room 10 at time T. The Purified Chalice is visible on the pedestal, enclosed in the Chronos Lock. There is no key in sight. The Temporal Key is not present at time T — it exists only at T+1, placed ahead by the room's design.

The natural first action is to attempt to take the Chalice. The defensive trap triggers immediately. The player learns, through this failure, that the Chalice cannot be taken directly.

The solution proceeds in four steps.

1. The player uses Door 12. Passing through it returns them to the center of the room and advances time to T+1. The Chronos Lock bound the Chalice to T, so at T+1 the Chalice is absent. The pedestal is bare. The defensive trap, having nothing to guard, is completely inert.

2. At T+1, the player finds the Temporal Key, which is present in the room at this moment. They also locate and disarm the trap mechanism on the pedestal — the physical or magical apparatus that the trap operates through. The trap cannot be triggered here because the Chalice does not exist at T+1, but the mechanism itself can be reached and permanently disabled.

3. The player uses the time-decrement mechanic — taught by Sekmidorgue as a theoretical lesson in preparation for this room — or another available means to return to time T. The specific mechanism for decrementing is provided by the lesson; this room is its first practical application.

4. Back at T, the Chalice exists again on the pedestal. The defensive trap mechanism has been disarmed; the trap no longer functions. The player uses the Temporal Key to open the Chronos Lock. The Chalice is retrieved without consequence.

The puzzle makes one principle concrete in a way that cannot be misunderstood: the Chronos Lock does not hide the Chalice. It pins it to a moment. In other moments, the Chalice is not hidden — it is simply not there. Acting at T+1 is not acting in secret. It is acting at a time when the thing being protected does not yet exist, which is a categorically different kind of absence than concealment. The trap cannot protect what is not present. The mechanism, however, persists across time and can be reached and disabled. The solution is a logical consequence of understanding this distinction rather than merely knowing that the distinction exists.

## Entry Flows

### Regular Entry

The player enters Room 10 from Room 5 via Door 13, having been briefed by Sekmidorgue on the subject of temporal locks and given a theoretical explanation of the time-decrement mechanic. The crystalline walls shimmer around them. The Purified Chalice is visible immediately on the central pedestal, enclosed in the Chronos Lock.

The player approaches the Chalice and attempts to take it. The trap triggers — a warning, a failure, a consequence. The room does not explain what just happened beyond the fact that something did. The player must interpret it.

Examining the Chronos Lock and the pedestal, the player understands that this lock is not conventional and that the Temporal Key they will need to open it is not present in the room at this moment. Door 12 is visible and labeled; its function — to loop back to the room's center while incrementing time by +1 — is discoverable either through examination or through use.

The player passes through Door 12. Time becomes T+1. The pedestal is empty. The Chalice does not exist here. The trap is dormant. The Temporal Key is now present in the room and can be retrieved. The player disarms the trap mechanism on the pedestal while it is unguarded.

The player uses the time-decrement mechanic to return to T. The Chalice is back. The trap mechanism is disabled. The player uses the Temporal Key to open the Chronos Lock and claims the Purified Chalice without consequence.

The player returns to Room 5 via Door 13 and presents the Chalice to Sekmidorgue. He examines it, confirms it is purified and correctly obtained, and delivers the final debrief of the tutorial phase. Door 14 is unlocked. The player passes through. Time increments by +1.

### Design Note

The room's question — *Can a moment be caged?* — is answered directly and without hedging: yes. The Chronos Lock is not a metaphor. It is a working mechanism that pins an object to a specific integer of time, making it inaccessible at every other moment. The implications of this are not contained to Room 10. Any object, anywhere in the game, that can be bound to a moment can be made effectively invulnerable for the duration of every other moment. Any defensive system built around an object's temporal presence can, in principle, be navigated by stepping outside the moment in which that presence exists.

This mechanic is not incidental to the main mystery. It is load-bearing. The tutorial teaches it here so that when the player encounters temporal locks in contexts where the stakes are higher and the reasoning is more obscured, they already hold the conceptual framework needed to work through what they are seeing.
