# Room 6

## Label

What becomes of the soulless?

## Description

A dimly lit stone annex carved directly beneath the Altar of Mages — a place that exists below the sanctified, above the forgotten. The walls are rough-hewn and cold, lit poorly enough that shapes resolve slowly as the player's eyes adjust. The contrast with Room 5 is immediate and intentional: the Altar above is vibrant, inhabited by mages who chose this life; the annex below holds those who had that choice stripped from them by their own actions.

The room serves as a combat and soul-purification tutorial. It is not a punishment chamber in the conventional sense — there are no torturers, no jailers, no pageantry of suffering. It is simply where the soulless end up. The label is answered the moment the player steps inside: two figures chained to the walls, barely recognizable as the mages they once were, existing without purpose, without will, without soul. This is what becomes of them.

The room was not built for cruelty. It was built by Sekmidorgue as a place of practical instruction — a controlled environment where a player with a pure soul can practice the mechanics of combat against creatures who cannot fight back effectively, and emerge purified for having ended what those creatures could not end themselves.

## Contents

### Creatures

**Chained Monster (x2)**

Former mages who committed too many cardinal sins and, over time, destroyed their own souls entirely. The process is irreversible. Whatever they were before is gone — their identities, their memories, their capacity for thought or feeling. What remains is a body that moves by instinct, capable of slow and telegraphed aggression, but nothing more.

Both are chained to the walls of the annex. The chains constrain their movement to a narrow arc, making their attack patterns predictable and their reach limited. They are not helpless — they will attempt to strike if approached — but they pose no real threat to a prepared player. They are ideal practice targets precisely because they have enough physicality to require genuine engagement, but not enough to punish mistakes harshly.

Killing them does not constitute a sin. These are not people. Their souls are gone, and what lingers is not a person but the residue of one. Ending them is, in fact, an act of purification: killing each Chained Monster purifies Bol's soul, incrementally strengthening it. The mechanical reward reinforces what the room teaches thematically — that these creatures should be released, and that releasing them is a mercy, not a violence.

### Artifacts

**Training Blade**

A standard physical sword provided by Sekmidorgue for use in this room. Its damage output is deliberately low and its stamina cost is very low — conditions chosen to encourage repeated engagement rather than overwhelming bursts. The design rewards players who practice chains of hits, who time their parries, who treat the combat as a discipline rather than a race to end it. It does what it is meant to do and nothing more.

**Wooden Shield**

A basic training shield, also provided by Sekmidorgue. Functional for blocking incoming strikes and practicing parries. Paired with the Training Blade, it gives the player both an offensive and a defensive tool, meaning all core combat mechanics — hitting, blocking, parrying, rolling, faking — can be practiced here before the player faces encounters with higher stakes.

### Doors

**Door 8 — to Room 5**

Two-way passage between Room 6 and Room 5 (the Altar of Mages). No time change on passage in either direction.

## Entry Flows

### Regular Entry

The player descends into Room 6 from Room 5 via Door 8. The shift in atmosphere is immediate — the warmth and light of the Altar give way to cold stone and dim shapes. The two Chained Monsters are visible from the entrance, secured to the walls on either side of the annex.

Sekmidorgue's training equipment — the Training Blade and Wooden Shield — is available in the room. The player equips both and engages the Chained Monsters in sequence or together, using the full range of available combat mechanics: hitting, blocking, parrying, rolling, faking. The creatures' constrained movement and slow attack patterns make them forgiving targets, suitable for building muscle memory without heavy punishment for early mistakes.

Defeating each Chained Monster purifies Bol's soul. Both must be killed to complete the room's purpose. Once both are down, the player returns to Room 5 via Door 8, carrying a strengthened soul earned through the annex below.

The label does not need to be explained. The player reads it at the entrance and the question is answered by what they see: two ruined former-mages, chained in the dark beneath the altar where the living practice their art above. The cost of losing one's soul is not abstract here — it is present, specific, and waiting in the room.
