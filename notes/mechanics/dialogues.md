# Dialogues — Who Killed Uskar?

This document contains key dialogue scripts and conversation flows for the game. Dialogues unfold the story and may branch based on player choices. Characters refer to the player's character using their known identity — the original vessel has no name, so characters either address them by a role-name (e.g. "prophet," "traveler") or avoid naming them at all.

---

## Lisspeg — First Meeting (Room 1, T=0)

**Context:** The player enters Room 1 as the unnamed original vessel. Lisspeg is the only creature present. He has been waiting here for a long time. He sees the player and immediately reacts.

**Tone:** Warm, simple, overcome. Lisspeg is not performing devotion — he is experiencing it. He is not smart enough to be suspicious and not selfish enough to ask for anything. His joy is the most uncomplicated thing in the room.

---

*Lisspeg looks up the moment the player enters. His face breaks into an expression of staggered relief — the kind that only comes after a very long wait.*

**LISSPEG:**
You came.

*He steps forward, hands clasped, eyes full.*

**LISSPEG:**
You actually came. I have waited so long in this room. I never doubted — not once, not for a single day — but still. To see it. To see you standing there.

*He steadies himself with a slow breath.*

**LISSPEG:**
Silarmousch the mage came to me in a dream. A real dream — the kind that doesn't fade when you wake, the kind that stays with you like a second skin. He told me to come to this chamber and wait. He said a prophet would arrive. Someone chosen. Someone who would carry me forward — toward the gods. He gave me this key and said: give it to the one who comes through that door.

*He produces the key — Silarmousch's Ward Key — and holds it out with both hands, as if offering something sacred.*

**LISSPEG:**
That is you. I know it is you.

*He waits. He is not testing the player. He simply believes.*

---

### If the player accepts the key:

**LISSPEG:**
Yes. Take it. It was always meant for you.

*He releases the key without any hesitation.*

**LISSPEG:**
The door at the far end — that key will open it. Beyond it, I'm told, is the way forward. I don't know more than that. Silarmousch said what was necessary and no more. But I trust it. I've trusted it all this time.

*He smiles.*

**LISSPEG:**
I am glad I waited.

---

### Optional Questions

*The player may ask optional questions before or after taking the key. Lisspeg answers each one without any change to his warmth or willingness.*

---

**[PLAYER CHOICE] "Who is Uskar?"**

*Lisspeg pauses. A small crease forms across his brow — not suspicion, just the effort of reaching for something half-heard.*

**LISSPEG:**
Uskar. I've heard that name. Somewhere. Someone said it once, a long time ago — I couldn't tell you who now. There was something sad about how they said it. Like someone who was lost.

*He shakes his head slowly.*

**LISSPEG:**
I don't know more than that. I'm sorry. Is Uskar someone you're looking for?

*He waits, genuinely hoping the player will tell him. He cannot be told anything that would disturb him.*

---

**[PLAYER CHOICE] "Who is Silarmousch?"**

*Lisspeg's whole face changes — not dramatically, but visibly. Something in him settles into reverence.*

**LISSPEG:**
A great mage. One of the greatest living, they say. He came to me in that dream and I knew — I just knew — that he was a figure of real power. Not the kind that shouts. The kind that doesn't need to.

*He leans forward slightly, confiding.*

**LISSPEG:**
He was kind to me. He explained everything calmly. He told me what was coming, what to do, what the key was for. Some people would have been frightened by a dream like that. I felt chosen. I felt like the luckiest creature alive.

*A beat.*

**LISSPEG:**
Is he known to you?

---

**[PLAYER CHOICE] "What is beyond the door?"**

*Lisspeg looks at the door — Door 1 — with the slight squint of a creature thinking carefully about something they actually know very little about.*

**LISSPEG:**
Forward. That's all I know. Silarmousch said it leads forward and that was enough for me. I didn't ask for a map.

*A small, self-deprecating laugh.*

**LISSPEG:**
Maybe I should have asked more questions. But I've found that when something feels true all the way down in your bones, asking too many questions just gets in the way.

---

*After any or all optional questions, Lisspeg returns to his quiet, waiting contentment. He has done what he was here to do. He has no further agenda and asks for nothing.*

## Bournache's Note (Room 3 — Central Reading Stand)

**Context:** The player enters Room 3 from Room 2, soul weakened by the murder of Lisspeg. The parchment sits on the central reading stand, glowing faintly. It is unmissable. This is the key exposition document of the early game — the text that explains what has happened to the player and what they can do about it.

**Tone:** Clear, urgent, and compassionate without sentimentality. Bournache writes as someone who knows their time is limited but refuses to be rushed. He wants to be understood. Every sentence earns its place.

---

*The parchment reads:*

---

You are reading this because you walked through a door that should not have been visible to you. Before you go any further, there are things you must understand.

**On souls and what they mean**

Every living creature in this world carries a soul. A soul is not a philosophical concept. It is a real thing with a real structure — a set of attributes that define who you are, how you perceive, and how the gods can reach you. The connection between your soul and the gods is what makes you a thinking being. Weaken it and you lose parts of yourself. Destroy it entirely and you become a monster: soulless, purposeless, still walking.

Soul soundness is measured. One hundred means whole. Zero means gone. In between, you are losing ground.

**On doors and what governs them**

Some doors in this world are only visible to certain souls. Divine doors open when your soul is pure above a certain threshold. They do not unlock — they appear. A creature with a wounded soul cannot see them at all. Demonic doors are the inverse: they become visible only when your soul has fallen below a threshold. The door you used to get into this room was a demonic door. It was invisible when you arrived in Room 2 with a whole soul. It appeared after you killed Lisspeg.

**On what was done to you**

You were set up.

The mage Silarmousch placed Lisspeg in that first room with a key and a story. He placed a dagger in the second room, in plain sight, the only thing there worth noticing. He designed a trap that required nothing of you except the simplest and most human choice: take the dagger, go back, use it. Most creatures who walk that path do not stop to think about why the dagger is there. Most don't need to be pushed. The situation pushes for him.

Murder is a cardinal sin. It corrupts the soul. The moment Lisspeg died, your soul weakened — and the demonic door became visible. The path into this room, into this library, into everything Silarmousch wants to show you. That is where you are now.

I want to be direct with you: Silarmousch is not your guide. He is steering you. I do not yet know all of his reasons, but I know what he has built here, and I know where it leads. Further down this path, your soul will weaken further. The divine doors will remain invisible. You will eventually reach a state from which recovery becomes very difficult, and which, taken to its end, leaves nothing.

**On the demonic door in this room**

There is a door in this room — Door 6 — that you can currently see and I cannot, because it is demonic. Do not take it. I am asking you, plainly: do not take it. What lies beyond it is Silarmousch's design, not yours. Walking through it is a choice in his direction, not your own.

**On what I am offering you**

There is a painting in this library called The Great Dawn. Use it. I have altered it to function as a signal — when you interact with it, I will know, and I will come.

After you have used the painting, advance time by one unit: take the return door out of this room into Room 2, and come back through. When you return, I will be here.

I am willing to offer my soul to you — a full and willing sacrifice. If you accept it, your soul will be purified completely. The sin of Lisspeg's death will not be undone, but its damage can be healed. When the purification is complete, Door 6 will disappear and a different door will become visible in its place. That door leads forward on a path Silarmousch did not build for you.

A willing sacrifice cannot be taken. It can only be given. I am giving it freely. I ask nothing in return except that you use what it costs me well.

I will explain more when we meet.

**On what knowledge is more accurate**

This room is full of books. They are worth reading. But a library built inside a trap is also part of the trap. Not everything here is false — some of it is genuinely useful — but it was curated with an intention you should keep in mind. You are standing inside something that was designed. Read accordingly.

*In hope — Bournache*

## Bournache — The Sacrifice (Room 3, T+1)

**Context:** The player has used The Great Dawn painting and advanced time by one unit (via Door 7 to Room 2 and back through Door 3). Bournache is now present in the library. He appeared at the reading stand, calm, as if he has been waiting — which, in a sense, he has. He is not surprised by anything.

**Tone:** Steady, warm without being soft. Bournache has made his decision and made peace with it. He is not trying to comfort the player — he is being honest with them. There is a clarity about him that comes from having already settled the hard questions before the conversation began.

---

*Bournache stands at the reading stand, one hand resting on the edge of it, facing the player. He does not move forward to close the distance. He simply is there, exactly as he said he would be.*

**BOURNACHE:**
You used the painting. I'm glad.

*He looks at the player carefully — not calculating, just present.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I know what you are. I have known since before you arrived in that first room. What is inhabiting you is older and larger than a creature, and I will not pretend otherwise. What you have been through in these first rooms — that was not an accident, and it was not your fault. You walked into something that was built to catch you.

*A beat.*

**BOURNACHE:**
You read the note. Then you know what I am offering and why. I have had a long time to consider this. It is not a sudden decision. I made it when I understood what Silarmousch had done here and what it would cost to undo it.

*He meets the player's gaze directly.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I am asking: do you accept?

---

### Player Choice: Accept the Sacrifice

**BOURNACHE:**
Then we are done deliberating.

*He steps slightly closer. His voice does not drop in volume — it just becomes quieter in quality, if that is possible.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I want you to understand one thing before I do this. Not for my sake — I am at peace — but because it matters for what comes after.

*He pauses.*

**BOURNACHE:**
Silarmousch designed this path because he knows something about what arrives in that first room. He knows you are coming and he has reasons for wanting you corrupted. I don't know all of those reasons yet. You will need to find them.

*He straightens.*

**BOURNACHE:**
Carry this forward. That is all I ask.

*He closes his eyes. The sacrifice begins — not violently. There is a shift in the air, a kind of stillness, and then the purification moves through the original vessel like a tide going out. When it is done, Bournache is gone. Door 6 has disappeared. On the far wall, for the first time, a door is visible that was not there before.*

---

### Player Choice: Refuse

*A brief pause. Bournache does not flinch or argue.*

**BOURNACHE:**
All right. That is your choice to make.

*He looks at the room around them — the books, the reading stand, the still-visible Door 6.*

**BOURNACHE:**
If you change your mind, use the painting again. I'll come back. I have time enough for that.

*He waits. If the player uses the painting again and advances time, the scene restarts from his arrival.*

---

### Optional Questions

---

**[PLAYER CHOICE] "Why are you doing this?"**

*Bournache considers how to answer — not because he doesn't know, but because he is choosing how much of the truth to give.*

**BOURNACHE:**
Because I believe the work matters.

*He pauses, then adds:*

**BOURNACHE:**
I have spent a long time watching what Silarmousch does and what it costs. He is very skilled at making harm look like guidance. There is no one else here who can counter what he built in these rooms — not in the way that's actually needed. If I can purify your soul and put you on the right path, that is worth more than whatever I would do with the rest of my years.

*He is not being self-pitying. He states it the way you would state a calculation.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I also believe that what you are carries the possibility of understanding something important about this world. Something that needs to be understood. I want to be part of how that becomes possible.

---

**[PLAYER CHOICE] "What happens to you?"**

*Bournache looks down for just a moment, then back up.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I will cease. The body goes. But a soul that is freely given does not disappear — it passes into whoever receives it. Something of me will travel with you. I find that more comforting than I expected.

*The faintest suggestion of a smile.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I am not frightened. I have been a mage long enough to understand what souls are and what they do. Mine has been a good one. I would rather it go somewhere useful than spend out quietly.

---

**[PLAYER CHOICE] "Who killed Uskar?"**

*For the first time, Bournache goes still. Not frozen — just paused, as though the question moved something in him that isn't usually moved.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I don't know the answer.

*He lets that sit for a moment before continuing.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I know the name. I know the weight of what that death caused. I know that it is not a simple story — there are too many forces arranged around it for it to be simple. But I do not know who did it, or precisely why, or what it means for everything that has followed.

*He looks at the player with something that might be called certainty, though it carries no arrogance.*

**BOURNACHE:**
I believe you will find it. That is part of why I'm here. You are going somewhere I cannot follow. The least I can do is make sure you get there.

## Bol — The Nectar Scene (Room 4)

**Context:** The player enters Room 4 as the original unnamed vessel, soul pure (purified by Bournache's sacrifice in Room 3). The room has no visible door and no weapon. Bol is present and approaches when the player enters.

**Tone:** Earnest and gentle, with a slight undercurrent of sadness. Bol was given his instructions by Bournache. He believes in what he is doing. He does not lie. He is, however, carrying something he does not entirely understand, and it shows not in his words but in the quality of his quiet.

---

*Bol moves toward the player steadily — not rushing, not hesitant. He has the manner of someone who has rehearsed this moment enough times that it no longer scares him, but not so many times that it has become rote.*

**BOL:**
I was told you would come.

*He stops at a respectful distance and holds the player's gaze.*

**BOL:**
My name is Bol. I'm a mage — or trying to be, at least. Bournache was my teacher. He sent me here before... before he went to do what he had to do. He gave me something to give to you, and he told me what to say.

*He produces the vial — small, ornate, the liquid inside carrying a faint luminosity.*

**BOL:**
He said: drinking this will open the way forward. That's what he told me to tell you, and I believe it. I've seen Bournache work. He doesn't do things without reason, and he doesn't send people into rooms without knowing what the room needs.

*He holds the vial out.*

**BOL:**
There's no door visible from here. I know that. But I was told this is the way.

---

### If the player takes and drinks the nectar:

*Bol watches without speaking as the player drinks. There is a moment — very brief — where something passes across his face that he controls quickly. Whatever it is, it is not duplicity. It is more like grief at something already decided.*

*Then the effect takes hold. The original vessel is gone.*

*[Scene transition: the ledger of souls appears.]*

---

### If the player asks what the nectar is:

**BOL:**
Something Bournache prepared. He didn't explain all of it to me — just said it was necessary, and that whoever came to this room would need it to go further. I trust that. I don't have the knowledge he had, but I know he wouldn't send me here with something harmful.

*A small pause.*

**BOL:**
He was careful about those things. Very careful.

---

### If the player refuses:

*Bol's expression doesn't become cold or impatient. He accepts the refusal with a kind of genuine helplessness.*

**BOL:**
Then we are both stuck. I don't know another way. I've looked. There's no door I can see, no other artifact, nothing else Bournache left here. Only this.

*He keeps the vial extended.*

**BOL:**
I'm not going to force you. But I don't know how to help you past this otherwise. I'm sorry.

*He waits. He will keep waiting, without pressure, for as long as the player needs.*

---

### If the player refuses and then asks again what the nectar is:

**BOL:**
I've told you everything I know. Bournache prepared it. It's necessary for the door. I don't know what it does beyond that — not precisely. He trusted me to deliver it, not to understand it fully.

*He looks at the vial.*

**BOL:**
That might not be reassuring. I know that. But it's what I have.

---

## Sekmidorgue — First Meeting (Room 5)

**Context:** The player has just entered Room 5 as Bol via Door 8 from Room 4. This is the first time Bol has been in the Altar under these circumstances — arriving as a vessel carrying an Outer God, following the death of Bournache. Sekmidorgue was expecting something significant. He recognizes the weight of what just arrived through that door.

**Tone:** Formal without being cold. Sekmidorgue speaks with the precision of someone who does not waste words. His authority is not performed — it is simply the way he occupies a room. He is Bol's guide now, and he takes that responsibility with complete seriousness. Warmth exists in him but it comes out through rigor, not softness.

---

*Sekmidorgue is near the center of the room when the player enters. He turns at the sound of Door 8. He does not move toward them. He takes in the situation for a moment — Bol, but not quite Bol — and something in his posture settles into a careful attention.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Bol.

*A pause. He looks at the player with the focused calm of someone confirming a hypothesis.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Something extraordinary is happening in this room. I can see it. An Outer God is present — not outside you, not beside you. Inside. You are carrying something the Altar of Mages has not seen in a very long time, if ever.

*He does not say this with alarm. He says it the way a cartographer notes an unexpected coastline: with attention and without hysteria.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
I have been expecting something since Bournache left. He did not explain everything to me — he rarely did — but he told me enough to know that whatever arrived here next would require careful handling.

*He glances toward the door the player came through.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
He is gone, then. I suspected as much when the signal came.

*Brief. He does not linger on it.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Then I am your guide now, Bol. And what is inside you is my responsibility as much as yours.

*He turns to fully face the player and settles his hands at his sides — a deliberate stillness.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Before you go any further, there are five things you need to know how to do. Five disciplines. I have rooms prepared for each of them. You will not pass through that door —

*He indicates Door 14, locked at the far end of the room.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
— until you have demonstrated all five. That is not a punishment. It is the only responsible way forward. What lies ahead will use everything you learn here. None of it is optional.

*He begins to list, with the methodical steadiness of someone who has taught these things many times but does not consider them routine:*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
First: combat and soul strength. Through Door 9, below us in the annex. Two former mages — they chose the demonic path far enough that nothing remained. You will fight them. You will learn what your soul does in a fight and what happens to it when you end something that was once a person but is no longer. This is not a pleasant lesson, but it is a necessary one.

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Second: magical paintings. Door 10, the gallery. A painting is not a depiction — it is a presence, an anchor between two places, and in the hands of a mage it becomes an instrument. Bournache used one to save your life. You need to understand how that is possible.

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Third: notes and written records. Door 11, the scriptorium. A note in this world is not merely an object. It carries mechanical weight. Written things can alter states, shift conditions, open paths. Understanding how they work is understanding how much of this world's history is actually accessible to you.

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Fourth: magical weapons. Door 12, the armory. A sword is not just a sword. Its power scales with the strength of the soul wielding it. The same blade in different hands produces different results. You will learn what that means in practice.

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Fifth: temporal locks. Door 13, the crystalline vault. Time in this world is not a river. It is a tree. Some things are locked to specific branches, specific moments, specific values. Understanding how to navigate around a Chronos Lock — how to move between time values to disarm something that seems impassable — this is the most difficult of the five disciplines and also the most important. I will not tell you why it matters most. You will learn that later.

*He pauses, giving the player a moment to absorb this.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
The order you complete these rooms is your choice. I recommend starting with combat — it is the most immediate and the most physical, and it will sharpen your focus for the disciplines that follow. But I will not force the sequence.

*One more beat. Then, directly:*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
You carry a god inside you, Bol. Do not waste the privilege.

*He moves slightly aside, opening sight lines to all five tutorial doors.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Begin when you are ready.

---

### After completing each tutorial room (brief debrief lines)

*When the player returns from each tutorial room, Sekmidorgue responds with a short acknowledgment before the player proceeds to the next.*

**After Room 6 (Combat):**

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
You engaged them fully. Good. Their kind cannot be reached by mercy — only by ending what is left. Your soul is stronger for it. That is the lesson.

---

**After Room 7 (Paintings):**

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
A painting is a window that a mage can walk through. You understand that now. The image is not the point. The connection is the point.

---

**After Room 8 (Notes):**

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Written things have weight. Be careful what you put into words. This world remembers writing in ways it does not always remember speech.

---

**After Room 9 (Weapons):**

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
The blade knows its wielder. This is not metaphor. Keep your soul sound and your weapon will tell you things about what you are fighting that nothing else will.

---

**After Room 10 (Temporal Locks):**

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
You moved through time to circumvent what could not be touched directly. Remember that. What is locked at one moment may not be locked at another. This applies to more than chalices.

---

### When all five rooms are complete and the Purified Chalice is returned

*Sekmidorgue examines the Chalice in Bol's hands. A long pause. Something in him is satisfied — not relieved, satisfied, which is a different quality.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
This is done correctly.

*He takes the Chalice, sets it down, and looks at the player.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Five disciplines. All demonstrated. What is inside you has shown that it can work through you without destroying you — not permanently, not yet. That is no small thing.

*He crosses to Door 14 and unlocks it.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Bournache said you were coming here for a reason that had nothing to do with combat or paintings or temporal locks. That the real work was somewhere ahead of all this.

*He looks back at the player from the door.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
I believe him. I believed him about most things.

*A pause — the only moment in all his dialogue that carries something personal.*

**SEKMIDORGUE:**
Go find out what he was right about.

*He steps aside. Door 14 is open.*
